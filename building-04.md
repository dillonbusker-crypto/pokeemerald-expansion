# Building 4 — "The Turn Order"

**Battle Tower, Link Multi counter.** Doubles, 6v6, level 50. BST cap 599. Six
battles drawn from twelve, then the boss.

**Design intent:** teach that Speed is something you *set*, not something you
have. Building 2 introduced Tailwind and Trick Room as one group among four.
Here they're the whole building, and every team has a plan for the turn order.

**Structural conceit:** four groups of three — Tailwind, Trick Room, terrain,
and speed reduction. The player who arrives with a static speed tier and no
plan loses six times.

| Group | Teams | Approach | What it punishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1–3 | Tailwind | Slow teams with no answer to a doubled speed tier |
| B | 4–6 | Trick Room | Fast frail teams, teams that can't survive five turns |
| C | 7–9 | Terrain | Ignoring field effects, priority reliance |
| D | 10–12 | Speed reduction | Assuming your speed tier is fixed |

**Counter naming.** This is the Tower's "Link Multi" counter in vanilla, which
expects a link cable and a partner. All of that is discarded — the player
should never see the word "Link."

---

## On the roster gap

Building 3's note flagged that the 551–599 band is nearly empty — Arcanine,
Florges, Archeops and Silvally are the only additions the cap raise brings.

**In practice this matters less than it looked**, for one reason: building 3
was singles. The player spent tier 3 buying singles Pokémon. Everything in the
501–550 band that defines competitive *doubles* — Incineroar, Rillaboom,
Togekiss, Hatterene, Grimmsnarl, Gholdengo, Porygon2, Tsareena, Dondozo — was
technically purchasable at tier 3 and almost certainly wasn't purchased.

So the shop has plenty to offer even though the cap barely moved. Building 4's
identity is the **format shift plus four new items**, not the cap.

Still worth deciding whether the ladder should be re-cut. But it isn't urgent.

## Bans

Permanent list applies: Smeargle, Wobbuffet, Shedinja, Ditto, Wishiwashi,
Palafin.

**Blaziken remains Blaze-only** in the shop. Speed Boost behind a Tailwind is
worse here than it was in singles.

**A limited set of legendaries arrives here** — the ones that won't break
building 5's singles once they're on the permanent roster: Entei, Raikou,
Suicune, Zapdos, Moltres, Articuno, Regirock, Regice, Registeel, Azelf, Uxie,
Mesprit, Terrakion, Cobalion and Virizion. Every other legendary, Paradox and
Ultra Beast in the 551–599 band stays held back.

The Forces of Nature stay out. Tornadus in particular is the best Tailwind
setter in the game and would trivialize group A — it arrives at building 7 as
designed.

**Pool teams carry at most one legendary each.** Ten of the twelve do; the two
Hatterene/Indeedee Trick Room teams (B4, B6) have none.

## Shop — building 4 additions

| Item | FP | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| Focus Sash | 400 | Guaranteed survival at full HP. Under reusable items this is permanent, every battle |
| Assault Vest | 350 | 50% Special Defense, at the cost of all status moves |
| Life Orb | 350 | 30% more damage for 10% recoil per hit |
| Heavy-Duty Boots | 300 | Ignores entry hazards entirely |

Pokémon at this tier: 400–600 FP.

**Focus Sash is the signature purchase and the one to watch.** In official play
a Sash is consumed and gone. Here held items restore after every battle, so a
Sash is a permanent guaranteed-survive in all seven battles of every run. That
is stronger than anything in competitive Pokémon.

The intended counter is multi-hit moves, which is why Weavile's Triple Axel,
Cetitan, Barraskewda's Population Bomb-adjacent options and Rock Blast users
appear across these teams. If Sash still feels oppressive, the fix is pushing
more multi-hit onto CPU teams rather than nerfing the item.

### The strategic dependency

Every set here is one a competitive player would run. The boss is not
handicapped — what it has is a **load-bearing condition**: the whole team is
built to move under Trick Room, and three of its six are slow enough that
without the room they move last against almost anything.

**But the two setters protect each other's weaknesses.** Hatterene's Magic
Bounce reflects Taunt and Encore. Indeedee's Psychic Surge blocks Fake Out and
every other priority move. The obvious answer to each setter is turned off by
the other one.

**That's the puzzle.** Taunt works on Indeedee but not Hatterene. Fake Out
works on Hatterene but not while Psychic Terrain is up. The player has to
either break the pair apart — remove one so the other becomes vulnerable — or
bring an answer that ignores both, which means their own Trick Room or enough
immediate damage to KO a setter outright.

**Tyranitar runs Sand Stream**, which chips the player's team and boosts its
own Special Defense by 50%. It's also a second pressure point: overwriting the
sand costs the boss real value, though unlike building 3 it doesn't break the
gameplan.

### Counterplay available at tier 4

- **Your own Trick Room** cancels theirs outright and is the cleanest answer.
  Porygon2, Dusclops, Oranguru and Hatterene all set it and are all purchasable
- **Taunt on Indeedee** works — Magic Bounce only protects Hatterene
- **Immediate damage on a setter.** Indeedee has 60/70 defenses and dies to
  most tier-4 offense if the player moves first
- **Good as Gold Gholdengo** ignores every status move on the team
- **Icy Wind and Electroweb** stack against Trick Room rather than with it —
  slowing an already-slow team makes it slower *inside* the room too
- **Tailwind** outlasts the room: five turns of Trick Room against four of
  Tailwind means the player controls the last turn

**Game plan.** Fake Out is off the table — Psychic Terrain goes up the moment
Indeedee leads — so the answer is to contest the room directly rather than
prevent it. Porygon2 sets its own Trick Room, cancelling theirs, and Trace
copies whatever ability it's facing. Weavile is fast enough outside the room to
remove Indeedee before Psychic Terrain does much work, and Triple Axel breaks
the Focus Sash on the way. Gholdengo's Good as Gold makes Hatterene's status
options inert. Icy Wind slows the pseudos whether the room is up or not.

**The lesson:** speed control isn't one tool, it's a category, and the answer
to one form of it is usually a different form rather than more speed.

## Open for playtesting

1. **Focus Sash under reusable items.** Flagged above and the biggest unknown
   in the building. If it's oppressive, add multi-hit moves to CPU teams rather
   than changing the item.
2. **The Magic Bounce plus Psychic Surge pairing on the boss.** Between them
   they turn off Taunt, Encore, Thunder Wave, Fake Out and all priority. That's
   most of the player's instincts at once. It should read as a puzzle with two
   separable halves — but if it reads as a brick wall, split the setters so
   only one is on the field at a time.
