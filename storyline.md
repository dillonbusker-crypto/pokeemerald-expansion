## The secret

### The Mega Pillar

The Battle Frontier's island sits on **Mega Stone ore**. Inside the **Battle
Factory**, which is no gauntlet but **Devon's control room** (Devon's
technology runs the island's systems), stands the **Mega Pillar**, a giant
Mega Stone receptor. The building is "Devon staff only", so the receptor is
never out in the open. Steven, a stone expert, notices Mega Stone ore in its
walls (gauntlet 2). The endgame plays out in its big rooms.

Mega Evolution runs on **bond energy**: the bond between a trainer and their
Pokémon, focused through a Key Stone. Every **counterfeit badge** is made of
an alloy seeded with **Key Stone shards**. Whenever a badge-wearer battles and
wins, the bond energy of that win flows through the badge into the Pillar.

### Everyone on the island wears them

**Scott only lets people onto the island if they carry counterfeit badges.**
That's what the Reception Gate is really checking. Every challenger on the
Frontier is feeding the Pillar.

### The backup: chipped Pokémon

Scott had a second channel built in from the start. **Every Pokémon from the
Frontier is implanted with the same shard material** as the badges: everything
sold at the Exchange Corner, Scott's five starting gifts, and the player's
starter too, because Birch got it from Scott.

The badges are far more effective at harvesting bond energy. The chips are the
backup: slower to charge the Pillar, but they work without the badges. When
Steven swaps the player's badges for real ones after gauntlet 4, the Pillar
keeps charging, just more slowly, and the player's Pokémon still don't grow.

### The one Pokémon without a chip

**The Latias or Latios from Artisan Cave** was caught wild. It never passed
through Scott's hands, so it has no chip. While the player wears counterfeit
badges, it's suppressed like everything else. **Once the badges are real, it's
the only Pokémon on the player's team that can gain Experience.** Steven uses
that to prove his theory (see gauntlet 4).

### What the badges and chips do, and hide

| What challengers think it is | What it really is |
|---|---|
| Every Pokémon is level 50 and never gains Experience | The shards take the growth. The energy that would have become Experience is harvested. |
| The L menu (stat editor, natures, move tools) | Birch built it so trainers can put power back by hand, and never notice their Pokémon stopped growing. |
| All eight badges, so any Pokémon obeys | The shards **force** obedience. It's an override, not trust. |

### Why Scott needs the player

Most challengers barely move the needle. Bond energy grows with how hard a
team is pushed and how long a streak runs, so **a trainer who keeps winning,
gauntlet after gauntlet, multiplies the harvest exponentially.** Scott has
scouted Hoenn for years looking for that trainer. It's the player, Norman's
child.

### What Scott wants: unlimited Mega Evolution

**Permanent Mega Evolution**, and permanent **Primal Reversion without the
orbs**. Pokémon that stay transformed with no stone, so every slot can hold a
real item. Scott stores the Pillar's harvest in a device he calls **the
Crown**: the bonds of thousands of trainers, in one hand. With it he means to
**overthrow the League, the Gyms and the Champion, and rule Hoenn.**

## Devon Corporation, the PokéNav, and the empty Pokémon Centers

**The full heal between battles was never a Frontier rule.** It's a Devon
feature built into the **PokéNav**, sold as making life easier for trainers.
It did: it made **Pokémon Centers obsolete.** Nurse Joys across Hoenn are out
of work and upset about it.

This is the first sign that Devon doesn't have people's best interests at
heart, long before the player learns what Devon built for Scott.

- A **Nurse Joy** can grumble about it early on 📝 (a Frontier Pokémon Center
  line).
- **If the player ever whites out** and wakes up in a Pokémon Center, the
  nurse is delighted: "Oh! You're the first patient we've had in a *long*
  time!" 📝

### The Scrambler 📝

A device that **scrambles the PokéNav's auto-heal**, so losing actually means
something and trainers can white out. It scrambles every PokéNav within a
short radius, including the one using it.

- **Flotsam uses one** when he betrays the player: "And don't think you can
  just auto-heal after this one, kid. I've scrambled your PokéNav. Mine too,
  but who's counting?"
- **Looker finds it** after Flotsam's arrest. He thinks Steven should look at
  it; the player mentions meeting Steven after gauntlet 2; Looker hands the
  Scrambler to the player.
- **After gauntlet 4**, the player shows it to Steven.
- **It's for story fights only.** The gauntlets keep their full heal between
  battles, a locked design decision. The Scrambler runs in the endgame's
  scripted fights: the Brains after gauntlet 8, Archie and Maxie, and Scott.

### The Pokémon Center closes

After gauntlet 8, **Nurse Joy refuses to help Scott or Devon**, so Scott's side
has nowhere to heal. With the Scrambler blocking their auto-heal too, anyone
the player beats **stays beaten.** Joy still helps the player ✅: she stands by
the Factory door while the player fights through it, and a white-out still
lands at her Center. Once Scott is gone she's back at her counter.

## The characters

### Scott

In the original games, Scott is a scout who watches trainers and never
battles. This story explains why.

- **Childhood:** wealthy, strict parents who called Pokémon "a waste of time"
  and forbade them. He grew up watching other children with their partners
  through a window.
- **Adulthood:** he became the man who watches, a scout and then the
  Frontier's founder. He surrounded himself with the strongest trainers in the
  world and **never had a partner of his own.**
- **The wound:** Mega Evolution needs a bond, and Scott has never bonded with a
  Pokémon. He can't Mega Evolve naturally, so he decided bond is a resource to
  **harvest** instead of feel.
- **The goal:** rule Hoenn with a team no one can answer, and decide who is
  allowed to train.
- **The flaw:** he's spent his life watching teams without understanding one.
  To him, the perfect team is the most powerful Pokémon in existence. **Raw
  power, no balance.**

**His house** ✅: instead of six polished Poké Balls, a single **Master Ball
on a pedestal**. Examining it before the ending gets Scott's reply: "A
replica. For show." It isn't. **Mewtwo is inside it.** It's gone from the
house once gauntlet 7 is cleared (see the event after gauntlet 7).

### Professor Birch

It began innocently. Birch wanted to know whether bond energy could be
harnessed, and Scott funded the research. When Birch tried to pull out, **Scott
threatened his child.** Everything Birch did in the opening is that threat at
work: the level 50 starter built with the L menu, the counterfeit badges, and
the "nobody wins by grinding levels" speech.

**Scott and President Stone both pressure him.** His deal with Devon was
better equipment for his lab in exchange for sending the kids to Scott, and for
finding out whether the Crown can actually work. The threats never stop, which
is why he's grown paranoid. He tells himself what they want is impossible, and
that he only needs time to prove it. He knows the harvest works (he built the
L menu to hide it); what he doubts is that the Crown can ever use it.
**Birch's full confession is a later beat, still to write.**

### President Stone

Devon Corporation built the Bond Harness for Scott. Scott promised Devon would
become the most successful company in the world, and that the harness would
send its shares through the roof. But Stone kept a failsafe: **a replica of the
Crown**, a second Bond Harness.

**Devon's technology runs the Frontier's systems**, the same way it runs the
PokéNav. Stone is **Steven's father**. Wallace sees him at Birch's lab after
gauntlet 5, so Steven knows his father is involved from then on.

### Norman

The player's father. Years ago, Scott tried to recruit him, and Norman refused.
Scott once threatened to "squash" him ✅, and says the same to the player at
the end. Held in the Factory with Mom after gauntlet 7; once rescued, he joins
Steven, Wallace and Wattson at Sky Pillar.

### Steven and Wallace

Steven is the former Champion and a stone expert. Wallace, the Champion, sent
him to find out why Juan left his Gym ✅. Together they investigate Devon, and
they're the player's allies for the whole endgame.

### The Wardens

Winona, Juan and Roxanne guard gauntlets 1–3 ✅. They joined for Scott's
promise of unlimited Megas, and **they stay loyal** through the endgame: they
**ambush Steven and Wallace outside Devon Corporation.** Wallace beats all
three.

Juan's "tell Wallace I'm sorry" ✅ foreshadows his real apology, which comes
outside Devon Corporation after that ambush.

### The Frontier Brains

Scott trained all seven. **Five are gauntlet bosses**: Anabel (4), Tucker (5),
Lucy (6), Spenser (7) and Brandon (8). **They stay loyal to the end.** After
gauntlet 8, five of them guard the Factory (Anabel, Tucker, Lucy, Noland and
Greta) while Spenser and Brandon hold off Wallace. They leave with Scott, and
**all seven ambush the allies outside Sky Pillar** at the end.

**Factory Head Noland and Arena Tycoon Greta are Scott's personal
henchmen**, with no gauntlet. They're the two men Scott sends into Artisan
Cave after the Lati is caught (replacing the generic men in suits). Greta is
rarely at her own building: the Arena is a sparring ground run by an
attendant ("The Tycoon's hardly ever around these days").

### Archie and Maxie

Scott promised them **permanent Primal Groudon and Kyogre** in exchange for
guarding the New Mauville generator. When they fail, he takes the legendaries
back.

### The Elite Four

Sidney, Phoebe, Glacia and Drake. Scott overpowered them, permanently
Mega Evolved some of their Pokémon, and promised them full access once he was
in control. They guard Devon Corporation. Wallace, the Champion, isn't one of
them.

### Flotsam, the Broker, Looker, the florist

The Artisan Cave side plot ✅ (`easter-eggs-spec.md`). Flotsam gains the
Scrambler 🔧. After the Factory, **the Broker runs the Exchange Service
Corner** with its four shopkeepers. The Factory's surge fried Devon's systems,
so everything is free, for the cause.

- **Looker works with Steven.** Chasing Flotsam is what brought him to the
  Frontier; that's why he hands the Scrambler on to Steven. After gauntlet 6
  he watches the Factory for Steven. ✅
- **The Broker** is an old friend of Steven's. After lending him the Metagross
  he starts digging into why Frontier Pokémon won't grow, which gets him
  **arrested after gauntlet 6** and held in the Factory (see below). ✅

### The Eon Duo, and what Scott lost

**Scott drew Latios and Latias to Artisan Cave on purpose.** The Soul Dew was
his bait. The player caught one and took the Soul Dew on the way out ✅. Scott
caught the other one later, and it's on his final team.

### Nurse Joy

Out of work since the PokéNav auto-heal. After gauntlet 6 she lets Steven and
the player use her empty Center as a hideout, and **overhears everything**,
which is why she later refuses to help Scott. ✅ In the endgame she brings every
Chansey and Blissey from Hoenn's Pokémon Centers, taught a new move, **Transfer
EXP** (one Pokémon at a time to level 100), and follows the player to Sky
Pillar.

### Wattson

Mauville's Gym Leader. When Aqua and Magma take over his town for Scott, he
leads the player into New Mauville, and he stands with Steven, Wallace and
Norman at Sky Pillar.

### Hoopa

Held in the Factory's Holding Block; the player frees it. Out of gratitude it
opens its rings to the strongest Pokémon in existence, three times
(see "Hoopa's rings").
