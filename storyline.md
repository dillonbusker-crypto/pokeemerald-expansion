# Ten Gauntlets — The Storyline

The whole story, start to finish. Companion to `frontier-dialogue.md` (the
world bible and the Frontier's dialogue), `easter-eggs-spec.md` (the Artisan
Cave quest) and `battle-tower-design-v2.md` (the gauntlets themselves).

**Status key:** ✅ built · 📝 planned · 🔧 built, but needs changing to fit
this version · ❓ open question

One ending. The earlier "break it or take it" choice is scrapped.

## The secret

### The Mega Pillar

The Battle Frontier's island sits on **Mega Stone ore**. Beneath the Hall of
Ten stands the **Mega Pillar**, a giant Mega Stone receptor. The **eleventh
plaque** at the head of the hall ✅ is its face: Mega Stone ore, not brass,
and blank.

Mega Evolution runs on **bond energy**: the bond between a trainer and their
Pokémon, focused through a Key Stone. Every **counterfeit badge** is made of
an alloy seeded with **Key Stone shards**. Whenever a badge-wearer battles and
wins, the bond energy of that win flows through the badge into the Pillar.

### Everyone on the island wears them

**Scott only lets people onto the island if they carry counterfeit badges.**
That's what the Reception Gate is really checking. Every challenger on the
Frontier is feeding the Pillar.

### The backup: chipped Pokémon

Scott had a second channel built in from the start. **Every Pokémon from the
Frontier is implanted with the same shard material** as the badges: everything
sold at the Exchange Corner, Scott's five starting gifts, and the player's
starter too, because Birch got it from Scott.

The badges are far more effective at harvesting bond energy. The chips are the
backup: slower to charge the Pillar, but they work without the badges. When
Steven swaps the player's badges for real ones after gauntlet 4, the Pillar
keeps charging, just more slowly, and the player's Pokémon still don't grow.

### The one Pokémon without a chip

**The Latias or Latios from Artisan Cave** was caught wild. It never passed
through Scott's hands, so it has no chip. While the player wears counterfeit
badges, it's suppressed like everything else. **Once the badges are real, it's
the only Pokémon on the player's team that can gain Experience.** Steven uses
that to prove his theory (see gauntlet 4).

### What the badges and chips do, and hide

| What challengers think it is | What it really is |
|---|---|
| Every Pokémon is level 50 and never gains Experience | The shards take the growth. The energy that would have become Experience is harvested. |
| The L menu (stat editor, natures, move tools) | Birch built it so trainers can put power back by hand, and never notice their Pokémon stopped growing. |
| All eight badges, so any Pokémon obeys | The shards **force** obedience. It's an override, not trust. |

### Why Scott needs the player

Most challengers barely move the needle. Bond energy grows with how hard a
team is pushed and how long a streak runs, so **a trainer who keeps winning,
gauntlet after gauntlet, multiplies the harvest exponentially.** Scott has
scouted Hoenn for years looking for that trainer. It's the player, Norman's
child.

### What Scott wants: unlimited Mega Evolution

**Permanent Mega Evolution**, and permanent **Primal Reversion without the
orbs**. Pokémon that stay transformed with no stone, so every slot can hold a
real item. Scott stores the Pillar's harvest in a device he calls **the
Crown**: the bonds of thousands of trainers, in one hand. With it he means to
**overthrow the League, the Gyms and the Champion, and rule Hoenn.**

## Devon Corporation, the PokéNav, and the empty Pokémon Centers

**The full heal between battles was never a Frontier rule.** It's a Devon
feature built into the **PokéNav**, sold as making life easier for trainers.
It did: it made **Pokémon Centers obsolete.** Nurse Joys across Hoenn are out
of work and upset about it.

This is the first sign that Devon doesn't have people's best interests at
heart, long before the player learns what Devon built for Scott.

- A **Nurse Joy** can grumble about it early on 📝 (a Frontier Pokémon Center
  line).
- **If the player ever whites out** and wakes up in a Pokémon Center, the
  nurse is delighted: "Oh! You're the first patient we've had in a *long*
  time!" 📝

### The Scrambler 📝

A device that **scrambles the PokéNav's auto-heal**, so losing actually means
something and trainers can white out. It scrambles every PokéNav within a
short radius, including the one using it.

- **Flotsam uses one** when he betrays the player: "And don't think you can
  just auto-heal after this one, kid. I've scrambled your PokéNav. Mine too,
  but who's counting?"
- **Looker finds it** after Flotsam's arrest. He thinks Steven should look at
  it; the player mentions meeting Steven after gauntlet 2; Looker hands the
  Scrambler to the player.
- **After gauntlet 4**, the player shows it to Steven.
- **It's for story fights only.** The gauntlets keep their full heal between
  battles, a locked design decision. The Scrambler runs in the endgame's
  scripted fights: the Brains after gauntlet 10, Archie and Maxie, and Scott.

### The Pokémon Center closes

After gauntlet 10, **Scott shuts the Frontier's Pokémon Center.** **Nurse Joy
refuses to help Scott or Devon**, so Scott's side has nowhere to heal. With the
Scrambler blocking their auto-heal too, anyone the player beats **stays
beaten.** Joy still helps the player.

## The characters

### Scott

In the original games, Scott is a scout who watches trainers and never
battles. This story explains why.

- **Childhood:** wealthy, strict parents who called Pokémon "a waste of time"
  and forbade them. He grew up watching other children with their partners
  through a window.
- **Adulthood:** he became the man who watches, a scout and then the
  Frontier's founder. He surrounded himself with the strongest trainers in the
  world and **never had a partner of his own.**
- **The wound:** Mega Evolution needs a bond, and Scott has never bonded with a
  Pokémon. He can't Mega Evolve naturally, so he decided bond is a resource to
  **harvest** instead of feel.
- **The goal:** rule Hoenn with a team no one can answer, and decide who is
  allowed to train.
- **The flaw:** he's spent his life watching teams without understanding one.
  To him, the perfect team is the most powerful Pokémon in existence. **Raw
  power, no balance.**

**His house** ✅: instead of six polished Poké Balls, a single **Master Ball
on a pedestal**. Examining it before the ending gets Scott's reply: "A
replica. For show." It isn't. It's the ball he uses on Rayquaza.

### Professor Birch

It began innocently. Birch wanted to know whether bond energy could be
harnessed, and Scott funded the research. When Birch tried to pull out, **Scott
threatened his child.** Everything Birch did in the opening is that threat at
work: the level 50 starter built with the L menu, the counterfeit badges, and
the "nobody wins by grinding levels" speech.

### President Stone

Devon Corporation built the Bond Harness for Scott. Scott promised Devon would
become the most successful company in the world, and that the harness would
send its shares through the roof. But Stone kept a failsafe: **a replica of the
Crown**, a second Bond Harness.

### Norman

The player's father. Years ago, Scott tried to recruit him, and Norman refused.
Scott once threatened to "squash" him ✅, and says the same to the player at
the end.

### Steven and Wallace

Steven is the former Champion and a stone expert. Wallace, the Champion, sent
him to find out why Juan left his Gym ✅. Together they investigate Devon, and
they're the player's allies for the whole endgame.

### The Wardens

Winona, Juan and Roxanne guard gauntlets 1–3 ✅. They joined for Scott's
promise of unlimited Megas, and **they stay loyal** through the endgame: they
**ambush Wallace in Rustboro.** Wallace beats all three.

Juan's "tell Wallace I'm sorry" ✅ foreshadows his real apology, which comes
outside Devon Corporation after that ambush.

### The Frontier Brains

Scott trained all seven. They're **loyal until the player beats them again**
after gauntlet 10. Then they turn: they **keep the Frontier island open for the
player** while the endgame plays out, so the player can always come back to
heal, swap teams and use the Broker's free shop.

### Archie and Maxie

Scott promised them **permanent Primal Groudon and Kyogre** in exchange for
guarding the New Mauville generator. When they fail, he takes the legendaries
back.

### The Elite Four

Sidney, Phoebe, Glacia and Drake. Scott overpowered them, permanently
Mega Evolved some of their Pokémon, and promised them full access once he was
in control. Wallace, the Champion, isn't one of them.

### Flotsam, the Broker, Looker, the florist

The Artisan Cave side plot ✅ (`easter-eggs-spec.md`). Flotsam gains the
Scrambler 🔧. After the Frontier falls, **the Broker takes in the escaped
shopkeepers** and runs the shop, free, for the cause.

### The Eon Duo, and what Scott lost

**Scott drew Latios and Latias to Artisan Cave on purpose.** The Soul Dew was
his bait. The player caught one and took the Soul Dew on the way out ✅. Scott
caught the other one later, and it's on his final team.

### Nurse Joy

Out of work since the PokéNav auto-heal. In the endgame she brings every
Chansey and Blissey from Hoenn's Pokémon Centers, taught a new move, **Transfer
EXP**, and follows the player to Sky Pillar.

### After gauntlet 4: Steven's test ✅

Steven comes back, in the Hall of Ten.

1. **The suspicion, confirmed.** There are Key Stone shards in the badges. While
   he carried the Rain Badge, **his own Pokémon stopped gaining Experience.**
2. **Real badges.** He takes the rest of the player's counterfeits and
   **replaces all eight with real ones**, arranged through Wallace and the
   Gyms. (Norman was right about the counterfeits.)
3. **The Scrambler.** The player shows him the one Looker handed over. He
   studies it.
4. **The test.** Steven notices the player's Latias or Latios: the one Pokémon
   on their team that came from the wild, not from Scott. He releases a weak
   wild Pokémon he caught nearby, an **Aron**, and has the player battle it with
   the Lati. **The Lati gains Experience.** Theory confirmed: the badges were
   holding it back, and because it was caught wild, nothing else is. But
   something is wrong with the **Frontier shop's Pokémon** too: Steven
   **borrowed a Metagross from the Broker**, and it gained no Experience even
   while he wasn't carrying the badges. He'll chase that down with Wallace
   (the chips, found at Devon).
5. **Lay low.** At this point the Metagross is more use to the player than to
   him. And a Lati that keeps growing would be a real edge through the
   gauntlets, but a Pokémon climbing past level 50 on Scott's island is exactly
   what would make him suspicious. **Scott seems to have taken a special
   interest in the player** and their progress through the gauntlets, so for
   now Steven needs the player to **keep progressing as if nothing has
   changed**, and see what Scott does next.
6. **The trade.** Steven holds on to the Lati for the time being, and gives
   the player the **Metagross, holding a Metagrossite** (OT "BROKER"). It isn't a
   choice. Steven is the most powerful trainer in Hoenn, and the player
   complies. Mega Metagross arrives just as the Mega Ring does (after gauntlet
   4), so it's usable right away.
7. **The Broker, after.** The next time the player visits him, he mentions
   Steven borrowing the Metagross, and their history: Steven once won the
   wildest bet the Gambler ever took, on beating the Battle Tower's Brain
   (`broker-spec.md`).

**The player always has a Lati by now.** The Artisan Cave quest is required:
gauntlet 4 stays locked until a Latias or Latios is caught.

**Where the Lati goes:** Steven keeps it safe until just **before gauntlet
10**, then gives it back with its Mega Stone (see the beat table). Scott's
Latias or Latios is still the *other* one, the one the player left behind in
the cave.

### After gauntlet 10: the Crown 📝

Scott is siphoning the Pillar into the Crown to build his team. **The seven
Frontier Brains guard him**, with upgraded teams.

This isn't a gauntlet. The player **picks them off one at a time**, in any
order, using the Scrambler. Scott has closed the Pokémon Center and Nurse Joy
won't help his side, so a beaten Brain can't heal and stays beaten. Joy still
heals the player between fights.

Each Brain, once beaten, turns: they keep the island open for the player for
the rest of the endgame.

**Scott's monologue.** His motives, his childhood, and that no one can stop
him: he's harnessed the bonds of thousands of trainers into the Crown. He
laughs at the idea of the player battling him: get out of the way, or he'll
squash them like he told their father he would. The player insists. "Fine. But
it's pointless. I don't play by the rules."

**The unwinnable battle.** Scott's whole team is **level 100**, and every one of
them is a permanent Mega:

| Pokémon | Notes |
|---|---|
| Mega Mewtwo Y @ Life Orb | The lead. Will likely sweep the player alone. |
| Mega Latias or Latios | The one the player left behind |
| Mega Kangaskhan | Parental Bond with an item |
| Mega Gengar | Shadow Tag |
| Mega Salamence | Aerilate |
| Mega Mawile | Huge Power |

Raw power, no balance, as always. The player loses. The story continues either
way; the script must not depend on the outcome.

**After the battle:** "Mewtwo came to me courtesy of Giovanni, in Kanto. And I
have **more to collect**." Scott flees, to catch **Rayquaza, Groudon and
Kyogre.**

### Steven and Wallace

They arrive after Scott leaves. While the player climbed, they investigated
Devon:

- **President Stone** was involved in developing the Bond Harness.
- **The chips:** every Frontier shop Pokémon carries the shard material, which
  is why the player's Pokémon still can't grow.
- They need a way to **disable the chips**, and Devon is the place to find it.
  Their own Pokémon can still grow, but even so, the two of them alone aren't
  enough to stop Scott.

The player, Steven and Wallace head to **Rustboro**.

### Rustboro: Devon Corporation 📝

**The Elite Four guard Devon**, their teams upgraded by Scott with permanent
Megas on some members. They keep **their real levels, up to 55**, because only
Scott's chipped Pokémon are stuck at 50.

- On the way in, **the three Wardens ambush Wallace**: Winona, Juan and
  Roxanne. "It's up to you to get past the Elite Four."
- Steven is off tracking Scott.
- The player beats **Sidney, Phoebe, Glacia and Drake.**

**President Stone.** Steven and Wallace join the player to question him. Scott
promised Devon would be the most successful company in the world, and that the
Bond Harness would send its shares through the roof. But Stone kept a
failsafe: **a replica of the Crown**, which he gives to the player. They don't
find a way to disable the chips.

**Outside, Juan.** He's with Wallace. Wallace tells the player he "whooped all
three of them" and tells Juan to get it together. **Juan apologizes**, to
Wallace and to the player.

**The scientist.** A Devon scientist runs after them: he overheard everything
and wants to help. In theory, the replica Crown could be **overclocked** to
disable the chips of every Pokémon in its holder's party. He upgrades it, but
it needs a massive power source.

### Mauville: New Mauville 📝

Steven and Wallace are on Scott's trail, so they send the player alone to the
power plant.

**Archie and Maxie block the entrance.** Scott gave them **permanent Primal
Groudon and Kyogre**. Two separate battles, **back to back, no healing** (the
Scrambler's doing).

The player reaches the generator and **overclocks the harness.** The player's
Pokémon can **gain levels** again.

**The bike:** the player picks up a **bike** from Mauville's cycle shop. They'll
need it to climb Sky Pillar.

### Back to the Frontier 📝

Steven and Wallace regroup with the player in Mauville. They tracked Scott to
the **Cave of Origin**, too late: he's heading for **Sky Pillar** to complete
his team.

Steven studies the overclocked harness. Something feels different about it. He
has an idea: take it back to the Frontier, where hundreds of shop Pokémon carry
shard chips, and **siphon the energy out of every chip at once.** It would free
those Pokémon and charge the harness.

At the Frontier:

- **The shop is closed.** Scott was careless and let the shopkeepers escape.
  **The Broker has taken them in**, and every Pokémon, item and Mega Stone is
  now **free**, for the cause. The player can freely come back to swap teams.
- The player uses the harness to **siphon the shards.** It's fully charged, and
  the player can now **permanently Mega Evolve.**
- **Nurse Joy** has heard the news. She's gathered every Chansey and Blissey
  from Hoenn's Pokémon Centers and taught them **Transfer EXP.** A Chansey at
  the Frontier's Pokémon Center can take the player's Pokémon **to level 100.**

The player prepares for the final battle.

### Sky Pillar 📝

The player, Steven and Wallace arrive. **The Elite Four ambush them.** Steven
and Wallace take them on so the player can climb. The player needs the bike for
the cracked floors.

At the top, Scott throws a **Master Ball** (the "replica") and **catches
Rayquaza.** He turns to the player. He's heard what happened at New Mauville
from Archie and Maxie. He'd promised them permanent Primal Groudon and Kyogre,
and took them back after their failure.

He activates the Scrambler. **This is life or death.**

### The final battle 📝

**Scott, level 100, every Pokémon permanently transformed, every one holding
an item, and no balance at all.**

| Pokémon | Why it's broken |
|---|---|
| Mega Rayquaza | Delta Stream, with an item on top |
| Primal Groudon | Desolate Land, taken back from Maxie |
| Primal Kyogre | Primordial Sea, taken back from Archie |
| Mega Mewtwo Y | Giovanni's, 194 base Special Attack |
| Mega Latias or Latios | The one the player left behind |
| Mega Kangaskhan | Carried over from his first team |

His first team's other three Megas (Gengar, Salamence, Mawile) make way for
the legendaries.

**Raw power only**, every set four attacks. **His own weathers fight each
other:** Desolate Land, Primordial Sea and Delta Stream each override the last,
so whichever legendary switches in cancels its teammates' advantage. It's the
unbalanced team made literal. A balanced team with pivots, speed control and
answers beats it.

**If the player loses:** they wake up at Sky Pillar. Nurse Joy tagged along to
help. She heals the team and sends the player back up to finish this.

**When the player wins,** Scott is arrested and the Crown is confiscated. A
happy ending.

## Technical notes for building it

- **⚠️ Trainer IDs will exceed the 1000 limit.** Rough count:

  | Content | Running total |
  |---|---|
  | Built now | 935 |
  | Gauntlets 7–10 (13 each) | 987 |
  | Seven Brain rematches, Scott ×2 | 996 |
  | Elite Four (Rustboro), Archie, Maxie | 1,002 |
  | Elite Four again at Sky Pillar, only if the player fights them there | — |

  Past the limit, a trainer silently shares its "defeated" flag with a system
  flag (see CLAUDE.md). Raise it **once, before release**, ideally when
  gauntlets 7–10 are built. It changes the save layout.

- **Vanilla maps come back into play:** Rustboro and Devon Corporation,
  Mauville, its cycle shop and New Mauville, the Cave of Origin, Sky Pillar.
  CLAUDE.md plans to strip the vanilla story; these maps must survive it, with
  their vanilla NPCs and trainers reworked. **Travel is by private transfer**
  between them, and each city is **blocked off** so the player can't wander
  out into the rest of Hoenn.

- **Levels.** Flat level 50 with no Experience is currently a test-only patch
  (`TESTING_DISABLE_EXP_GAIN`, CLAUDE.md). The story needs a real rule instead:
  a Pokémon gains Experience only if **the harness has been overclocked** (New
  Mauville), or if **the badges are real and it isn't chipped** (the Lati, from
  Steven's gauntlet 4 test on). "Chipped" means it came from the shop, Scott or
  Birch, which needs a marker on each such Pokémon.
- **Steven's test battle** is a wild battle (a scripted Aron), so it costs no
  trainer ID.
- **The required quest costs 900 FP of the 1,200 gauntlet 3 pays:** a
  Leafeon (300) and the Master Ball (600). ✅ Built: while the quest is
  unfinished, the island's "POS is down" and every other FP sink is closed
  (see `easter-eggs-spec.md`).
- **Swapping the Lati for Metagross** is a scripted trade: remove the Lati from
  the party or PC, give a Metagross holding a Metagrossite, and later give the
  Lati back holding its stone. Steven's handover needs to find the Lati
  wherever it is.
- **Steven's Metagross** carries a Metagrossite. Mega Metagross is 700 BST,
  above gauntlet 5's 600 cap for Megas: a deliberate, story-given edge. The Chansey's Transfer EXP needs a special that sets the party to
  level 100 and recalculates stats.

- **Permanent Megas and Primals.**
  - Enemy: trainer Pokémon can be listed directly in Mega or Primal form
    holding a normal item. Verify they don't revert on switching.
  - Player: real engine work. Forms normally revert on switching and after
    battle. Needs investigating before it's promised.

- **No-heal chains:** scripts simply don't heal between the battles. A loss
  whites out to the last checkpoint with a healed team, and the whole chain
  must be redone.

- **The unwinnable Scott battle:** the story continues on a win or a loss.
  Check a level 50 team can't realistically win, and that winning doesn't
  break anything.

- **The free shop:** a story flag that zeroes shop prices once the Broker takes
  over.

- **Every opponent** needs a real class and pic, and anything fought under
  Frontier rules needs `IsFacilityBattleTrainer()` coverage (see CLAUDE.md).
