# Ten Gauntlets — The Storyline

The whole story, start to finish. Companion to `frontier-dialogue.md` (the
world bible and the Frontier's dialogue), `easter-eggs-spec.md` (the Artisan
Cave quest) and `battle-tower-design-v2.md` (the gauntlets themselves).

**Status key:** ✅ built · 📝 planned (this document is the plan)

## The secret

### The receptor

The Battle Frontier's island sits on **Mega Stone ore**. Artisan Cave is cut
through it, and the **eleventh plaque** at the head of the Hall of Ten isn't
brass like the other ten. It's a slab of that ore, and it's a **giant Mega
Stone receptor**.

Mega Evolution runs on **bond energy**: the bond between a trainer and their
Pokémon, focused through a Key Stone. Every **counterfeit badge** is made of
an alloy seeded with **Key Stone shards**. Wherever a badge-wearer battles and
wins, the bond energy of that win flows through the badge into the receptor.

### Everyone on the island wears them

The player isn't the only one. **Scott only lets people onto the island if
they carry counterfeit badges.** That's what the Reception Gate is really
checking. Every challenger on the Frontier is feeding the receptor.

### What the badges do, and hide

The Frontier's "rules" are the badges at work:

| What challengers think it is | What it really is |
|---|---|
| Every Pokémon is level 50 and never gains Experience | The badges take the growth. The energy that would have become Experience is harvested. |
| The L menu (stat editor, natures, move tools) | Birch built it so trainers can put power back by hand, and never notice their Pokémon stopped growing. |
| All eight badges, so any Pokémon obeys | The Key Stone shards **force** obedience. It's an override, not trust. |
| A full heal between every battle | **The badges heal**, running on stored bond energy. Every challenger depends on it without knowing. |

### Why Scott needs the player

Most challengers barely move the needle. They lose, they stall, they never
push their Pokémon hard. Bond energy grows with how hard a team is pushed and
how long a streak runs, so **a trainer who keeps winning, gauntlet after
gauntlet, multiplies the harvest exponentially.** Scott has scouted Hoenn for
years looking for that trainer. It's the player, Norman's child.

### What Scott wants: unlimited Mega Evolution

**Permanent Mega Evolution.** Pokémon that stay Mega with no stone, so every
slot can hold a real item. A full party of six Megas holding Life Orbs and
Choice items would be the strongest team that has ever existed. Scott is the
only one who can make it, and he gatekeeps it. With it, he means to
**overthrow the League, the Gyms and the Champion, and take Hoenn.**

## The characters

### Scott

In the original games, Scott is a scout. He travels Hoenn watching trainers
and never battles. This story explains why.

- **Childhood:** wealthy, strict parents who called Pokémon "a waste of time"
  and forbade them. He grew up watching other children with their partners
  through a window.
- **Adulthood:** he became the man who watches: a scout, then the Frontier's
  founder. He surrounded himself with the strongest trainers in the world and
  **never had a partner of his own.**
- **The wound:** Mega Evolution needs a bond, and Scott has never bonded with a
  Pokémon. He can't Mega Evolve, and never will naturally. So he decided bond
  is a resource that can be **harvested** instead of felt.
- **The goal:** take Hoenn with a team no one can answer, then decide who is
  allowed to train. It's his parents' rule turned inside out, with him as the
  one saying no.
- **The theme** of every battle against him: **stolen bond against real
  bond.** His Megas are permanent, perfect and hollow.

Hints already in the game ✅: his house's six polished Poké Balls ("…One of
them twitches." after gauntlet 10), the eleventh plaque ("SCOTT. — Undefeated
—" after gauntlet 10), two NPCs who've never seen him battle, the Brains' "Has
Scott ever told you who taught all of us?", and Norman's warning.

### Professor Birch

It began innocently. Birch wanted to know whether bond energy could be
measured, stored and redirected, and Scott funded the research. When Birch saw
what Scott meant to do with it, he tried to pull out, and **Scott threatened
his child.**

Everything Birch did in the opening is that threat at work: he built the
player's level 50 starter with the L menu, handed over the counterfeit
badges, and gave the "nobody wins by grinding levels" speech with his own
child standing there, keeping them safe by keeping them ignorant.

### Norman

The player's father. Years ago, Scott tried to recruit him as the strongest
trainer he'd scouted, and Norman refused. Using his child is both Scott's
next-best option and his way of settling that score. Norman has "heard
rumors" ever since.

### Steven

The former Champion and a stone expert, sent by Wallace to find out why
Juan left his Gym. He spots the counterfeit badges and the Mega Stone
plaque. Later he finds his own family's company in the supply chain: **Devon
Corporation refined the ore and made the badge alloy**, with Mr. Stone's
approval, as "energy research".

### The Gym Leaders turned Wardens

Winona, Juan and Roxanne guard gauntlets 1–3. They joined because Scott
promised them access to permanent Megas, and **they jump ship** once they see
what it's for. Juan's "tell Wallace I'm sorry" ✅ is the first sign.

### The Frontier Brains

Scott trained all of them. They're loyal, and in the dark about the receptor.
Some start to doubt as the player climbs.

### The rival

Wanted a fair fight since Route 103 ✅. Later discovers they were the leverage
Scott held over their father.

### Flotsam, the Broker, Looker

The side plot ✅ (`easter-eggs-spec.md`). Flotsam is a small-time crook who
stumbled into Scott's plan. Looker is investigating the island on his own
initiative, because a trainer with counterfeit badges doesn't officially
exist.

### The Eon Duo, and what Scott lost

**Scott drew Latios and Latias to Artisan Cave on purpose.** The island's
energy attracts them, and the **Soul Dew** was the bait he planted to keep
them there. He meant to collect both once the receptor was full.

Then the player walked in, caught one, and **took the Soul Dew on the way
out** ✅. The other Lati flew deeper into the cave, and Scott caught it later.
At the finale, his Mega Latias or Latios is the one the player left behind:
"You took something of mine in that cave. Two things, actually."

### Making the charge visible 📝

Each gauntlet cleared, the badges' descriptions change ("…faintly warm",
"…humming", "…glowing"), and the eleventh plaque's text shifts. Players feel
the harvest building long before the reveal. This uses the same
gauntlets-cleared reactivity as the rest of the Frontier.

## Gauntlet 11: "The Receptor" 📝

Beyond the eleventh plaque. **The ten bosses, back to back**, then Scott.

Scott has given each boss a taste of what he's selling: **two permanent Megas
each, and Uber-grade teams.** Each keeps their own format (singles or doubles),
with the usual full heal between battles, because the badges still heal.

Each boss has a different reason to stand in the player's way:

| Boss | Why they fight |
|---|---|
| Winona, Juan, Roxanne | **Testing, not stopping.** "If you can't beat us at full power, you can't beat him. We have to be sure." They took Scott's Megas to train the player. Juan finally explains himself. |
| Anabel | **Loyal but doubting.** After losing: "…He never told us what the plaque was for." |
| Tucker | **Loyal and dazzled.** Permanent Megas are the ultimate show. He doesn't see it until he loses. |
| Noland | **Curious.** He engineers teams, and wants to see what a perfect one looks like. |
| Lucy, Greta, Brandon | Decided when gauntlets 7, 8 and 10 are built. |
| Spenser | Already knows the truth. Decided with gauntlet 9 whether he fights at full strength or throws the match. |

**The power gap is the point.** The player has at most one Mega per battle;
every opponent has two permanent ones. The player's answers are Uber access
in the gauntlet 10 shop, and a real bond: their Mega evolves mid-battle and
adapts; the opponents' can't.

### Scott

**Six permanent Megas, every one holding an item, and no balance at all.**

Scott's flaw is the lesson of the whole game. He spent his life *watching*
teams without ever understanding one. To him, the perfect team is simply the
six most powerful Pokémon in existence, all of them Mega, all of them holding
the strongest items. It's raw power, top to bottom: no pivots, no speed
control, no hazard removal, no recovery, no Protect, no defensive answers.
Four attacks on everything.

Every team the player built to get here worked because it was *balanced*.
Scott never learned that. His chase for unlimited Megas wasn't as good as he
thought, and the battle proves it.

| Pokémon | Held item | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Mega Rayquaza | Life Orb | Delta Stream, plus an item on top |
| Mega Mewtwo Y | Choice Specs | 194 base Special Attack, with Specs |
| Mega Latias or Latios (the one the player left behind) | Choice Scarf | Personal, and fast |
| Mega Kangaskhan | Choice Band | Parental Bond, twice, with a Band |
| Mega Mawile | Expert Belt | Huge Power |
| Mega Gengar | Wise Glasses | Shadow Tag traps the player in with it |

Distinct items throughout, so the item clause holds. Every set is four
attacks.

**How it loses.** Terrifying on paper, and every hit is a knockout threat, but
it has nothing to fall back on:

- **Choice locks** are punishable: switch in a resist, Protect, or bait a
  Choiced attack into an immunity.
- **No speed control** of its own, so Trick Room, Tailwind, paralysis and
  priority all turn the fight.
- **No recovery and no pivoting**, so chip damage, Rocky Helmet, weather and
  hazards stack up; once something is worn down, it stays worn down.
- **Shared weaknesses:** Mewtwo, the Lati and Gengar all fall to Dark and
  Ghost; several of the team fear Fairy and Ice.
- **No answer to setup:** a single safe Dragon Dance or Swords Dance on a
  bulky, well-built Pokémon can sweep a team that only knows how to attack.

A balanced team, one with pivots, speed control and answers, beats it. That's
the point.

**After the battle,** Scott doesn't understand why he lost: "I had the
strongest six Pokémon that have ever existed. Every one of them Mega. How…?"
The player's team, the one they *built*, is the answer.

This is also the contrast for ending B. Steven's two teams there are the
opposite of Scott's: several permanent Megas each, but **deliberately
balanced**, which is exactly what makes them harder.

The battle is **forced**. There's no offer to join him first.

## The choice

After Scott falls, the receptor hums at full charge. The player chooses.

### Ending A: Break It

The player smashes the receptor. **Every counterfeit badge on the island
crumbles**, and the power is gone for good.

Norman, the rival and Steven arrive to find it already done. Looker arrests
Scott, whose Pokémon are ordinary again and never bonded with him. **Mom
arrives** too: "I told you, if anything happens, you can always come home…
We should have given you the adventure you deserved in the first place."

The eleventh plaque now reads the player's name. The game ends.

### Ending B: Take It

The player claims unlimited Mega Evolution: their Pokémon stay Mega
permanently and hold items. The story continues.

**1. Witnessed.** Norman, the rival and Steven arrive in time to see it. They
flee without a word. Scott: "They'll come around. Or they won't. Either way —
we finish the job."

**2. Devon Corporation.** Meanwhile, Steven goes home to Rustboro and **fires
his father, Mr. Stone**, for his part in the badge alloy. He puts Devon's
engineers to work on something.

**3. "Something's wrong in Rustboro."** Scott sends the player by **private
transfer**, straight off the island with no routes and no HMs.

- **The Elite Four and Champion Wallace guard Devon Corporation:** Sidney,
  Phoebe, Glacia, Drake, then Wallace.
- Their teams are **Uber-grade with normal Megas** (one per battle). Only
  Scott and Steven ever have permanent Megas.
- The player wins, and learns they were buying time.

**4. The Stonebreaker.** Devon's engineers built Steven a device that
**breaks the Key Stone shards in every counterfeit badge**, which also cuts
off the badges' healing. He has fled to **Mossdeep's Space Center** to launch
it into orbit and disable every fake badge in Hoenn at once.

**5. Mossdeep Space Center, first floor.** The Stonebreaker is already active
inside. **No healing between battles**, but the player's permanent Megas still
work.

- **Norman**, then **the rival**. Two separate battles, back to back, no heal
  between them.
- The family the player walked away from, and the rival who wanted a fair
  fight since Route 103.

**6. Second floor.** Steven at the launch console.

- "I thought I could trust you. I never took you for the power-hungry type."
- Devon's engineers also built him a permanent-Mega device, in case it came to
  this.
- **Steven, battle 1:** several permanent Megas but not a full team of them.
  Balanced, and as strong as it gets.
- "All these trainers only carry six Pokémon. I came prepared." **Steven,
  battle 2:** a completely different team, also with several Megas. No heal in
  between.

**7. Last resort.** Steven has rigged the Space Center to blow. Just before he
triggers it, **Mom walks in** and talks them both down: "I told you, if
anything happens, you can always come home… We should have given you the
adventure you deserved in the first place."

**8. Reconciliation.** Everyone makes up. **The player hands Scott over to
Looker**; that's their redemption, and Scott's team goes with him. The game
ends.

### Losing a no-heal chain

The player respawns at **the last checkpoint with a healed team**, and must
redo **the whole back-to-back chain**, even if they won its first battle. On
the Space Center's first floor, that's Norman and the rival again; on the
second, both Steven battles. This is how difficulty scales at the end: by
chaining battles, not by adding more of them.

## Technical notes for building it

- **⚠️ Trainer IDs will exceed the 1000 limit.**

  | Content | Running total |
  |---|---|
  | Built now (gauntlets 1–6 plus story trainers) | 935 |
  | Gauntlets 7–10 (13 each) | 987 |
  | Gauntlet 11 (ten bosses) plus Scott | 998 |
  | Ending B (Elite Four, Wallace, Norman, rival, Steven ×2) | ~1,007 |

  Past the limit, a trainer silently shares its "defeated" flag with a system
  flag (the gauntlet 4 bug; see CLAUDE.md). The limit must be raised **once
  more, before release**, ideally when gauntlets 7–10 are built. It changes
  the save layout, so saves from before it stop loading, which only matters
  during development.

- **Rustboro (Devon Corporation) and Mossdeep (the Space Center) are vanilla
  maps.** CLAUDE.md plans to strip the vanilla story eventually. These maps
  must survive that, with their vanilla NPCs and trainers cleared out.

- **Enemy permanent Megas:** trainer Pokémon can be listed directly in their
  Mega form holding a non-stone item. Verify they don't revert on switching
  before designing around it.

- **The player's permanent Megas (ending B)** are real engine work. Forms
  normally revert on switching and after battle. Needs investigating before
  it's promised.

- **Turning off the heal** needs no engine work. Those scenes simply don't
  heal, and a loss sends the player back to the checkpoint.

- **Every opponent from here on** needs a real class and pic, and anything
  fought under Frontier rules needs `IsFacilityBattleTrainer()` coverage (see
  CLAUDE.md).
